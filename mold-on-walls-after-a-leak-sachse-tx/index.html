Mold on Walls After a Leak in Sachse, TX: What to Do

By the Sachse Water Damage Pros teamPublished 2026-10-26Updated 2026-10-26

Mold on a wall is a sign that the wall stayed wet. This guide explains how to find the moisture, what you can clean and when Texas rules call for a licensed pro.

Mold on walls after a leak in Sachse is a moisture problem first and a cleaning problem second. If the spot is small, dry and clearly on the surface, you can clean it under Texas rules. If it covers a large area, sits behind a leaking fixture or keeps returning, stop and call a pro. Do not scrub or bleach until you know where the water came from.

Why walls grow mold after a leak

Drywall paper is organic, and it holds water long after the surface feels dry. A leak behind a wall or above it can keep the back of the drywall damp for weeks, while the front looks fine. Mold then grows on the paper and spreads along it.

The state insurance department warns that mold may start within a day of a leak. That is why drying matters so much. If a leak was cleaned up quickly and dried, mold usually never starts.

Find the moisture before you clean

  • Check the source. Look for a leak in the plumbing, the roof or an air conditioner drain line.
  • Use a moisture meter. A damp wall reads higher than a dry one.
  • Look for staining. Brown rings suggest past water.
  • Smell the area. A musty smell often marks hidden moisture.
  • Compare with another wall. The difference tells you how wet it is.

If the wall reads wet, cleaning will not hold. Dry the area first with air movers and a dehumidifier, which is the work described on the water mitigation page.

What you can clean and what you should not

Small patches on hard, painted surfaces that are dry can be cleaned with detergent and water, then dried completely. Wear gloves and eye protection, and keep the area contained so you do not spread dust through the house.

Do not clean mold on porous materials such as drywall paper that is crumbling, insulation or carpet. Those are usually removed. Do not mix cleaning chemicals, and do not use a household vacuum on mold, since it can spread particles.

The Texas 25 square foot rule

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation states that areas of visible mold under 25 contiguous square feet can be cleaned by people who are not licensed. For 25 contiguous square feet or more, remediation must be done by a licensed contractor, and a notification is required.

A licensed company cannot both assess and remediate the same project. That rule keeps the final clearance check independent. Ask any company for its TDLR license number before you sign.

Testing and clearance

If the mold is more than a small patch, or if you want confirmation of what it is, a licensed assessor can inspect, sample and write a protocol. After the work, the assessor checks that the area meets the plan. Your lender or a buyer may ask for that paperwork.

Health questions about living in a home with mold belong to your physician, and we do not make health claims.

What a good repair looks like

The crew finds and fixes the source, dries the cavity, removes material that cannot be cleaned and documents the work with photos and readings. New drywall goes in only after the framing reads dry. Primer and paint finish the wall.

Ask for the written scope and for a statement of what was removed. Do not accept a plan that only covers the surface. Painting over a damp wall hides the problem and delays the repair.

Common questions about timing

Mold can begin within a day after a leak, according to the Texas Department of Insurance, but visible growth often takes longer to appear. A wall that looked fine last month may show spots now. If you recently had a leak and did not dry it, check the wall.

Sachse notes: slab homes and leaks

Basements are uncommon in Texas, according to KUT reporting, and slab foundations are common in North Texas. Water that enters a Sachse home usually comes from plumbing, a roof or an air conditioner drain, and it spreads through walls and ceilings more than through a basement.

The City of Sachse maintains water lines up to the meter, so a leak behind a wall on the house side is a private repair. Permits for rebuilding walls are handled by Development Services at 469-429-4781.

When to call

Call if the mold covers more than a small patch, if it returns after cleaning or if you see it in more than one room. Related pages explain water damage cleanup for removing wet materials and mold remediation for the full process.
