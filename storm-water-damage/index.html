Emergency Storm Water Damage Response in Sachse, TX

A storm does not leak in one place. Wind pushes rain sideways through window frames and garage doors, hail can open shingles, limbs can crack a roof, and runoff can pool at a threshold. The damage inside can have three or four causes at once.

For a single leak that comes straight from the roof, our roof leak water damage page walks through the attic and ceiling path. This page is for the broader aftermath of a storm.

How storms damage Sachse homes

The National Weather Service normals for DFW show about 37 inches of rain a year, so a hard storm can drop a large share of that quickly. The December 2015 tornado is a reminder that severe weather reaches this area, and the NWS reported an EF-4 track through Sunnyvale, Garland and Rowlett.

Muddy Creek runs through the Sachse area, and USGS lists a drainage area of 28.1 square miles for its gage. Heavy rain upstream can raise the creek and local runoff can pool at low spots.

What you see and what it means

  • Water around window sills and door thresholds. Wind-driven rain got past the seals or the ground water reached the entry.
  • Wet carpet near an exterior wall. Water traveled along the slab edge or the wall base.
  • Ceiling stains in more than one room. The roof may have more than one opening.
  • Damp garage walls and a soaked floor. Runoff came in under the door or along the slab.
  • A musty smell a day later. Moisture is still trapped in insulation, carpet pad or drywall.

First-hour safety steps

  • Wait until lightning and wind have passed before going outside or onto a roof.
  • Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets, cords or the panel. Call 911 or the electric utility for electrical risk.
  • Watch for downed lines and limbs near the house.
  • Cover broken windows or roof openings with a tarp or board if it is safe, or call a contractor.
  • Photograph every wet room, damaged roof area and entry point.
  • Call our line for the inside work, and your insurer to report the loss.

How we handle storm water

Water type comes first. Rain through a roof and groundwater are not the same, so a technician checks where it entered. Standing water is extracted with portable units and submersible pumps. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera find water in walls and ceilings.

Wet carpet pad and insulation are removed when they cannot dry. Air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers dry the rest. The reference for the work is the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, and readings are recorded each visit.

What changes the cost

The number of entry points, the type of water, how long it sat, how many rooms and floors are wet and what the surfaces are made of all change the scope. Contaminated ground water costs more to handle than clean rain through a roof.

A technician explains scope and price first, and drying days follow readings.

Storm damage and your insurance

The Texas Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies cover sudden wind and hail damage but generally exclude flood from rising water, which needs a separate policy. Rain that enters through a storm-made opening is often treated differently from groundwater.

Document the storm, the roof, the entry points and the interior. If a contractor offers to waive your deductible, know that Texas law prohibits it.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting for the adjuster before drying. Insurers expect reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • Going onto a wet roof. It is dangerous, and a contractor can inspect it safely.
  • Using a generator indoors. Exhaust fumes are deadly.
  • Throwing out wet items before photographing them.
  • Assuming a dry-looking wall is dry. Moisture hides in cavities.
