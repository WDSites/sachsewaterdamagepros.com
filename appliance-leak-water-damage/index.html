Appliance Leak Water Damage Cleanup in Sachse, TX

A washer hose or dishwasher line can leak while the house is empty. We find where the water went, dry the floor and the cabinet bases, and document the loss.

Most appliance leaks start small and end up larger than they look. A washer hose can work loose behind the machine, a dishwasher gasket can drip into the cabinet base and a refrigerator ice maker line can seep for weeks. By the time a floor looks wet, the water has often already gone under cabinets and into the subfloor.

This page covers leaks that begin inside an appliance or its connections. If the room is the bigger issue, such as wet cabinets and flooring around a sink, see our kitchen water damage repair page.

Where appliance leaks come from

Washers lose water through supply hoses, drain hoses and worn fittings. Dishwashers leak at the door gasket, the pump housing or the supply connection. Refrigerators leak through the ice maker line or the water filter housing. Water heaters and air handlers have their own pages because the damage pattern is different.

The Census Bureau counts about 84.7 percent of Sachse homes as owner-occupied. For most households the appliance, the supply line and the water damage that follows all belong to the person who lives there.

What you see and what it means

  • A soft or swollen spot at the base of a cabinet. Water is wicking into particleboard.
  • Cupped or lifting floor planks beside an appliance. Water got under the flooring.
  • A musty smell near the laundry room. Moisture is trapped in a wall base or under flooring.
  • A water stain on a wall behind a washer. A supply connection has been dripping.
  • A higher water bill with no explanation. A hidden leak may be running.

First-hour safety steps

  • Close the appliance's own water valve. It is usually on the wall behind or beside the unit.
  • Unplug it only if the floor around the plug is dry. Otherwise leave the power alone and call the electric utility or 911 for electrical risk.
  • Pull the appliance out only if you can do it without stepping into water.
  • Soak up surface water with towels and lift rugs and boxes off the floor.
  • Call our line before the water dries. Drying marks fade, but moisture remains.
  • Photograph the hose, the appliance and the wet floor.

How we handle appliance leaks

The technician maps the water with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, then follows it under cabinets and along baseboards. Surface water is extracted with a portable unit. Toe kicks and baseboards are removed so air can reach the wet cavity.

Air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers dry the area. Flooring and cabinet bases are checked for swelling, and materials that cannot recover are removed. Readings are logged each visit. The reference for the work is the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard.

What changes the cost

The appliance location, how long it leaked, whether water got under cabinets or into a neighboring room, the flooring type and the number of drying days all change the scope. A tile floor on a slab dries more easily than engineered wood over a subfloor.

Scope and price are explained before any work begins.

Appliance leaks and your insurance

The Texas Department of Insurance says a standard homeowners policy generally covers sudden and accidental water damage and not gradual leaks. A hose that bursts is usually sudden. A seep that went unnoticed for weeks or months may be excluded.

Note the date you first saw the leak, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Pushing the appliance back into place while the wall is wet. The leak stays hidden.
  • Drying only the visible floor. Water in the cabinet base is the part that causes the damage later.
  • Reusing a cracked or old hose. A new hose costs little next to a flooded floor.
  • Ignoring a small damp patch. It tends to grow.
  • Skipping a shutoff check. Many valves seize if they are never turned.
