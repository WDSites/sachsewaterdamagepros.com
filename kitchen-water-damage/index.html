Kitchen Water Damage Repair in Sachse, TX

Kitchens hide water under cabinets and behind appliances. We dry the cabinet bases, flooring and walls, then help you sort what can stay from what must be replaced.

☎ (972) 945-2885

Kitchens combine plumbing, appliances and cabinets in a small space. A leak under the sink drips into a cabinet box, a dishwasher drain puts water under the toe kick and a refrigerator line leaves a puddle behind the unit. The water stays out of sight until the wood swells.

This page is about the room and the materials in it. If the cause was an appliance, our appliance leak water damage page covers the failed hoses and connections.

How kitchens get wet in Sachse homes

The Census Bureau reports a median owner-occupied home value near $406,200 in Sachse, and kitchens are often the most expensive room to rebuild. Cabinets, counters and flooring carry real value, which is why drying properly matters.

Most kitchens here sit on a slab, so water that gets under cabinets and flooring has nowhere to go but sideways. The city handles water mains up to the meter, and everything past that point, including the shutoff under the sink, belongs to the homeowner.

What you see and what it means

  • A swollen or soft cabinet base. Particleboard has absorbed water.
  • A lifted or cupped floor plank near the sink or dishwasher. Water is under the flooring.
  • A musty smell when the cabinet doors open. Moisture is trapped under or behind the box.
  • A stain on the ceiling below an upstairs kitchen. A leak passed through the floor.
  • Peeling paint or soft drywall behind the backsplash. Water has wicked up the wall.

First-hour safety steps

  • Close the shutoff valve under the sink or behind the appliance.
  • Unplug nearby appliances only if the floor around the plug is dry.
  • Stay out of water that touches outlets or cords. Call 911 or the electric utility for electrical risk.
  • Empty the cabinet and set items on dry ground.
  • Soak up the surface water and lift rugs and boxes.
  • Photograph the cabinet, the pipes and the floor.

How we dry a kitchen

The technician maps moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera, since cabinets and appliances hide the wet area. Surface water comes out with a portable extractor. Toe kicks come off so air can reach the cavity under the cabinets.

Air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers dry cabinets, flooring and walls. Wet backsplash or drywall may be opened when readings stay high. Readings are logged each visit, and the reference for the work is the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard.

What changes the cost

The number of cabinets reached, the flooring material, whether the water got under the cabinets or into walls, how long it sat and how many days of drying the readings call for all change the scope. Tile and slab dry more easily than wood over a subfloor.

You get the scope and the price in plain terms before work starts.

Kitchen water damage and your insurance

The Texas Department of Insurance explains that standard homeowners coverage generally covers sudden and accidental water damage and not gradual leaks. A burst supply line is usually sudden, while a drip under a sink that ran for months may not be.

Keep the failed part, take photos of the cabinet and floor and report early.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Wiping the floor and leaving the cabinet wet. Water in the box is the long-term problem.
  • Installing new flooring over a damp subfloor. It can cup and grow odor.
  • Sealing the toe kick back too soon. Moisture is trapped again.
  • Ignoring a small leak at a supply line. It often returns.
  • Painting over a stain before drying. The stain comes back.
