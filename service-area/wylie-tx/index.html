Lavon Lake and the East Fork Trinity

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers describes Lavon Lake Dam as about 3 miles east of Wylie on the East Fork Trinity River, built for flood control, water supply and recreation. A major reservoir close to town affects storm planning and flood maps. Lavon Lake does not rule out local flooding, because a heavy rain can still flood low streets and yards. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about a separate flood policy.

Two counties, one dispatch

The overlap can mean paperwork with two county offices. Ask your insurer which county your parcel lists.

When water is spreading in Wylie

  • A line is running inside the house. Close the main valve and call.
  • Storm water is coming under a door or up through a drain. Stay out of the water if outlets are nearby and call the utility or 911 for electrical risk.
  • A water heater is leaking at night. Shut off the cold water supply to it and power to the unit if you can do so safely.
  • Ceilings are stained after a storm. Keep clear of the sag and set a bucket on the floor away from the center.
  • You are not sure how bad it is. Call anyway. The 24/7 line takes questions.

Water supply and permits in Wylie

Wylie is one of the original member cities of the North Texas Municipal Water District, which describes itself as a wholesale provider of water, wastewater and solid waste services. NTMWD sells to member cities rather than to residents, so call the City of Wylie for a main break in the street.

For repair permits, the City of Wylie has a Building Inspection department. Ask whether your drywall, flooring and cabinet replacement needs one before the rebuild begins.

The February 2021 freeze affected the region, and NTMWD asked customers to curtail non-essential use from February 17 until February 25, 2021. Pipes frozen in that week often failed on thaw.
