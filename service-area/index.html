Water Damage Service Areas Around Sachse, TX

Crews are dispatched from Sachse to four nearby towns. Each town has its own utility, flood exposure and ZIP codes, so each has its own page.

Home base: Sachse, TX 75048

Our address is 6406 S State Highway 78 in Sachse, TX 75048. Sachse sits on State Highway 78 in far northeastern Dallas County and southern Collin County, and it had an estimated 32,834 residents in 2025 according to the Census Bureau. The city buys its treated water from the North Texas Municipal Water District and maintains water mains up to each meter, as the water mitigation page explains.

Nearby towns we cover

Garland is the large city southwest of Sachse, with about 2,400 acres of 100-year floodplain along Duck, Rowlett and Spring Creeks. Its page covers ZIP codes 75040 through 75044 and the city's flood rules.

Wylie lies to the north and east, with Lavon Lake dam about three miles to its east. Its page covers ZIP 75098, the county split and the water supply arrangement.

Rowlett sits south of Sachse on Lake Ray Hubbard. Its page covers ZIP codes 75088 and 75089 and storm history.

Lavon is a small Collin County city where water comes from Bear Creek Special Utility District instead of the city. Its page covers ZIP 75166 and who to call for each utility.

What changes from one town to the next

Water damage looks the same on a ceiling in any town, but the people you call and the risks you face differ. Garland and Wylie are member cities of the North Texas Municipal Water District, so their treated water comes from the same wholesale source as Sachse. Lavon's customers are served by Bear Creek Special Utility District instead, which matters when you need to find the shutoff or report a main break.

Flood exposure differs too. Garland reports about 2,400 acres of 100-year floodplain, and Rowlett borders Lake Ray Hubbard. Lavon's flood ordinance adopts the Collin County flood insurance study dated June 2, 2009. Each town page lists the details that apply to its addresses.

Before you call

  • Know the address. Give the street and ZIP so dispatch can confirm coverage.
  • Shut off the water if you can. Locate the main valve and turn it off.
  • Say what the water looks like. Clean, gray or sewage changes the plan.
  • Photograph the damage. Take pictures before anything is moved.
  • Check your policy. Insurance rules come from your policy and the Texas Department of Insurance.

Licensing and permits across town lines

Mold work is regulated by the state. The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation licenses mold assessors and remediators, and a company cannot do both on one project. Building permits are local. Sachse requires one for construction or repair of any building, and Lavon's building inspection office can be reached at 972-843-4220. Ask the city for your address before you plan a rebuild.

Addresses that sit on a boundary

Town lines and ZIP codes do not always match. Wylie's 75098 ZIP is also used with Lucas and St. Paul mailing names, and Sachse itself spans two counties. If your mailing address names one town while your home sits in another, tell dispatch both. The utility, the permit office and the flood map for your property follow the city and county where the house stands, not the name on your mail.

This matters most for sewer backups and main breaks, because the city or utility responsible is the one that covers your lot. Sachse sends after-hours sewer backups to Police Dispatch at 972-495-2271, option 2. Other towns have their own numbers, which you can find on their pages.
