When a pipe lets go

A burst or thawed pipe releases a lot of water fast. The first priority is the main valve, then the electrical risk, then calling for help. A crew can extract standing water once the line is isolated, even before a plumber arrives.

Our burst pipe water damage and frozen pipe damage restoration pages explain the steps in detail.

Safety before cleanup

  • Close the main valve only if you can reach it safely.
  • Avoid standing water near outlets or appliances. Call 911 or the electric utility for an electrical risk.
  • Do not use household vacuums on standing water.
  • Move valuables and rugs from wet floors when it is safe.
  • Photograph the damage before anything is moved.
  • Treat sewage or dirty water as a hazard and call a professional.

What cleanup involves

Cleanup begins with finding how far the water went. A technician reads floors, wall bases and ceilings with moisture meters, then removes standing water and anything that cannot dry. Air movers and dehumidifiers work until the readings show the structure is dry.

Drywall, insulation and carpet pad are judged one by one. Some can stay and some must go, and we will say which and why.

Insurance

The Texas Department of Insurance explains that sudden and accidental water damage is generally covered and gradual leaks are not. A burst pipe is often sudden, but policies differ. We cannot promise coverage, and a contractor may not waive or absorb your deductible.
