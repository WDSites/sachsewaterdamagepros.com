Carpet, pad and subfloor

Water Damaged Carpet Restoration in Sachse, TX

Wet carpet can often be saved if it is dried quickly and the water was clean. We extract, lift and dry it, and tell you plainly when the pad or the carpet has to go.

Carpet is the first thing most people notice when a room floods. It soaks up water quickly, holds it in the pad and passes it to the subfloor. The surface can feel only damp while a quart sits underneath.

This page covers carpet and pad. For wall damage after the same leak, see wall and drywall water damage repair.

How carpet gets wet in Sachse homes

Burst lines, appliance leaks, roof leaks and storm water are the usual sources. In many homes here, carpet sits over a concrete slab, which means water can spread sideways under the pad and stay trapped.

The Census Bureau reports a median owner-occupied home value near $406,200 and about 84.7 percent owner-occupied housing. Carpet is a major interior finish, so the save-or-replace decision matters to most homeowners who call.

What you see and what it means

  • A squishy feel when you step. The pad is holding water.
  • A dark edge along a wall. Water has wicked into the tack strip or the baseboard.
  • A musty smell that returns after the surface dries. The pad or the subfloor is still wet.
  • Ripples in the carpet. The backing is loosening from the pad.
  • Stains that appear on carpet in a spot you did not wet. Water traveled under the floor.

First-hour safety steps

  • Stop the water source at the shutoff valve or the main.
  • Stay away from wet outlets and cords. Call 911 or the electric utility for any electrical risk.
  • Lift furniture legs and place foil or blocks under them to stop stains.
  • Remove small rugs and wet items to a dry spot.
  • Open windows if the weather allows and run the fan, not the central heat.
  • Photograph the carpet before cleanup and call our line.

How we restore wet carpet

The technician maps the wet area with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera. Surface water is extracted with a weighted tool that pulls water out of the pad as well as the carpet. A corner is lifted so air can reach the pad and the subfloor.

If the carpet can be saved, it is lifted and dried over the pad or the pad is replaced. Air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers dry the room, and moisture readings confirm it. The ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard is the guide for how the work is judged.

What changes the cost

The area that is wet, the carpet and pad type, how long it sat, whether the water was clean and how many days of drying the readings call for all change the job. Carpet over a slab dries more easily than carpet over a wood subfloor.

The technician goes over scope and price with you before the crew starts.

Carpet water damage and your insurance

The Texas Department of Insurance explains that sudden and accidental water damage is generally covered and gradual leaks are not. Carpet replacement is a common line in a claim.

Keep photos, readings and receipts. They show the carpet was handled promptly.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving wet carpet to air dry. Pad and subfloor stay wet far longer than the surface.
  • Using a household vacuum. It is not meant for water and can be a shock risk.
  • Saving carpet that touched sewage. It should be replaced.
  • Walking on wet carpet. It spreads water and loosens the backing.
  • Covering a wet carpet with new flooring. Moisture is trapped and odor follows.
