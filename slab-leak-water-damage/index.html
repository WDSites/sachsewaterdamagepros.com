Emergency Slab Leak Cleanup in Sachse, TX

A leak under a concrete slab shows up as warm floors, damp carpet and a rising water bill. We dry the flooring and walls it reached while a plumber finds and fixes the line.

☎ (972) 945-2885

When a supply line under a slab leaks, the water has nowhere to go but up and sideways. It travels along the bottom of the concrete, finds a crack or a joint and appears under carpet, wood flooring or baseboards. Sometimes it never shows until the floor feels warm or the water bill climbs.

If a line in the attic or wall split, our burst pipe water damage page applies instead. This page covers cleanup after water from under the slab reaches the living space.

How slab leaks happen in Sachse homes

North Texas houses are often built on concrete slabs. Reporting from KUT and KERA notes that basements are uncommon in Texas and slab foundations are common in North Texas. Plumbing lines run under or through the slab, and the soil around them moves.

The Texas Water Development Board describes the state soil, Houston Black clay, as a Blackland Prairie soil whose range runs from north of Dallas toward San Antonio and which shrinks and swells markedly with moisture. Soil movement of that kind can stress buried pipes and slab edges, though no source here ties any one Sachse leak to it.

What you see and what it means

  • A warm spot on a floor. A hot water line under the slab may be leaking.
  • The sound of running water with every tap closed. A line is open somewhere.
  • Damp carpet or a musty smell without a source. Water is rising through the slab.
  • A water bill that jumps with no change in use. A hidden leak is running.
  • Baseboards swelling or flooring lifting. Moisture is wicking from below.

First-hour safety steps

  • Close the main water valve and see whether the meter stops moving.
  • Stay away from wet outlets and cords. Call 911 or the electric utility for any electrical risk.
  • Lift rugs and furniture legs off wet floors.
  • Do not break into the slab yourself. Pressurized lines and gas lines can run nearby.
  • Call a plumber for leak detection and our line for the water.
  • Photograph wet areas and the meter reading.

How we handle slab leak damage

The technician uses moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to map where water has risen. Standing water is extracted with portable units. Wet carpet pad is removed, and baseboards come off so wall bases can dry.

Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers run until readings approach a dry reference. Flooring that cannot recover, such as glued wood or swollen laminate, may need to come up. The work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference.

What changes the cost

How much of the floor is wet, what it is made of, how long the leak ran, whether walls were reached and how many days of drying the readings call for all change the job. Wood and carpet over a slab cost more to handle than tile.

The line repair itself is the plumber's scope and is priced separately.

Slab leaks and your insurance

The Texas Department of Insurance explains that sudden and accidental water damage is generally covered and gradual leaks are not. A slab leak can be gradual by nature, which makes the timeline matter.

The leak, the water damage and any foundation movement can sit under different parts of a policy. Keep the plumber's report, the leak detection findings and the readings together for your agent.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Covering a damp floor with new flooring. Moisture is trapped and the problem returns.
  • Ignoring a warm floor spot. A hot line leak can run for a long time.
  • Skipping the plumber's leak detection. Guessing leads to extra holes in the slab.
  • Turning the water on and off repeatedly to test. It spreads the leak.
  • Waiting until a bill arrives. The damage keeps building in the meantime.
