When standing water becomes an emergency

  • More than a few puddles. Water you can see across a room has already reached the baseboards.
  • A pipe, hose or water heater is still running. Stop the source first and call right after.
  • Water is touching outlets, appliances or the electrical panel. Do not wade in. Call the utility or 911 for electrical risk before calling us.
  • Sewage is coming out of a drain or toilet. Stay out and do not run any more water in the house.
  • The ceiling is sagging. Water trapped above can release all at once, so keep everyone clear.

Tools that pull water out of a house

Extraction is mechanical work. Truck-mounted or portable extraction units create suction through wands that lift water from carpet and hard floors. Submersible pumps move deeper water through hoses to a drain or the yard. Wet vacuums finish edges and corners.

After the bulk of the water is gone, a technician checks moisture in the subfloor, baseboards and lower wall sections. Carpet is sometimes lifted and the pad removed so the floor underneath can dry. Everything that was touched by water is measured, because standing water that looked shallow often traveled much farther inside the walls.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is the classic reason for a midnight call. A split supply line can release a house worth of water in an hour, and the first priorities are the same every time: close the main valve, open a faucet on the lowest floor to relieve pressure, and call.

Extraction is the first step, not the last

Removing standing water does not make a house dry. Wood, drywall and insulation hold water long after the floor looks clear, and that trapped moisture is what leads to warped flooring and mold.
