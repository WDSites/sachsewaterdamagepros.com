Water damage in Sachse homes: what is different here

Sachse sits on State Highway 78 in far northeastern Dallas County and southern Collin County. The city buys its treated water from the North Texas Municipal Water District, and the city maintains water mains and service lines up to your meter. Anything on the house side of the meter, including a supply line, a water heater or an ice maker line, is yours to repair with a licensed plumber.

That split matters at 2 a.m. If water is running inside the house, the shutoff you want is usually the main valve at the house or the meter. If a sewer line is backing up, the city handles the main and the lateral up to your property line. For an after-hours sewer backup, the city directs residents to Police Dispatch at 972-495-2271, option 2.

Basements are uncommon in Texas and slab foundations are common in North Texas, according to KUT's reporting on Texas foundations. Water from a failed supply line or a leaking dishwasher therefore spreads across flooring and into the bottom of the drywall instead of pooling below the house. A slab leak also hides under flooring for weeks, which is why a musty smell and warm spots on the floor deserve a call.

Muddy Creek runs through the area, and the city keeps Muddy Creek Preserve at 5400 Pleasant Valley Road. A USGS gage on the creek near Creek Crossing Lane tracks a 28.1 square mile drainage area. If your home sits near a creek corridor, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about a separate flood policy, because standard homeowner policies exclude rising water.

Winter pipes | What the February 2021 freeze taught Sachse homeowners

On February 16, 2021, DFW reached minus 2 degrees, and the freeze held for 139 hours. The North Texas Municipal Water District asked customers across the region to cut non-essential water use from February 17 until February 25, 2021, and warned that thawing would cause more ruptured pipes.

The practical lesson is about timing. Pipes often split while frozen but only leak once they thaw, so the damage shows up days later in a ceiling or behind a wall. DFW averages its first freeze around November 22 and its last around March 12, so the window for that kind of loss runs through most of winter.

Water categories | How the type of water changes the job

Water damage is not one job. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories, and the category decides what can be dried and what must be removed.

Category Typical source What it means for the job
1 Broken supply line, tub overflow, ice maker line Clean at the source. Dry quickly and most materials can stay.
2 Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine only Contaminated. Porous materials such as carpet pad often come out.
3 Sewage, rising floodwater, long-standing water Heavily contaminated. Wet porous materials are removed and surfaces sanitized.

Water can move up a category the longer it sits, which is why a clean supply line leak left for several days is treated differently from the same leak stopped in an hour.

Scope and price | What decides the cost of a Sachse water loss

  • Water category. Clean water is the least costly to handle and sewage the most.
  • Affected area. Square footage of wet flooring and the height water wicked up the walls.
  • Materials. Tile dries easily, while carpet pad, hardwood and drywall absorb and may need replacing.
  • Drying days. Moisture readings decide how many days of air movers and dehumidifiers the structure needs.
  • Rebuild scope. Replacing drywall, flooring and cabinets is priced separately from the dry-out.

We do not publish flat prices because two homes with the same leak rarely cost the same. After the technician measures, you get the scope and price explained before work starts, then you decide. Call (972) 945-2885 and describe the damage.
