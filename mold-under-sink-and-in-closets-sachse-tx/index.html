Mold Under the Sink and in Closets in Sachse, TX

By the Sachse Water Damage Pros teamPublished 2026-10-26Updated 2026-10-26

Cabinets and closets hide moisture for months. This guide covers why mold shows up there, how to check each spot and what to do about it.

Mold under the sink and in closets usually points to a slow moisture source nearby: a drip from a fitting, condensation on a cold pipe, a drain line overflow or a damp shared wall. Cleaning the visible spot will not hold unless that source is fixed and the area is dry. Check the cabinet floor, the pipe connections and the wall behind first.

Why these two spots

Cabinets and closets have something in common. Both are enclosed, both get little airflow and both sit against walls that carry plumbing or air conditioning lines. A small amount of water in a closed space stays damp far longer than the same water in an open room.

Leaks that stay wet for a day can be enough for mold to take hold, according to the state insurance department. Under a sink, that clock starts with any steady drip. In a closet, it starts when a wall behind the shelf stays wet.

Check under the sink

  • Touch each connection. Hold dry paper against supply valves, traps and the disposal.
  • Look at the cabinet floor. Swelling, warping or dark staining shows long exposure.
  • Smell the cabinet. A musty odor points to ongoing moisture.
  • Run the water. Watch the drain for slow weeping while it runs.
  • Check the wall behind. Staining can travel into the wall cavity.

Remove stored items first and photograph the area. If the particle board floor of the cabinet is soft, plan to replace it, since particle board does not recover from soaking.

Check the closet

Pull items away from the walls and look at the base of each wall, the baseboard and the carpet edge. Check the ceiling above for stains. A closet on an exterior wall can sweat if the air conditioner runs while outside air is humid, and a closet near a bathroom may share a plumbing wall.

Use a moisture meter if you have one. Compare the closet wall to a wall in another room. A reading well above the baseline means water is present even if nothing is visible.

What to do about it

Small, dry patches on hard surfaces can be cleaned with detergent and water, then dried completely. Texas rules allow unlicensed cleanup of visible mold under 25 contiguous square feet, according to the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Anything larger, or any mold on soft materials that cannot be dried, needs a licensed remediator.

Do not mix cleaning products and do not seal damp wood with paint. Painting over a damp surface hides the problem and often peels within weeks.

When to call a pro

Call if the wood is soft, if the spot returns after cleaning, if more than one room is affected or if the wall behind the cabinet or shelf is wet. A crew can dry the cavity, remove affected material and document it, as described on the water mitigation page.

A company that assesses mold cannot also do the cleanup on the same project under Texas licensing rules, which keeps the final check independent.

Preventing a repeat

Replace worn supply lines, tighten slip nuts and check the garbage disposal seal. Keep cleaning products on a shelf rather than directly on the cabinet floor, since leaks from containers add moisture. A small tray under the trap can catch drips early.

Look under the sink monthly. A flashlight and a dry paper towel take a minute, and they catch small weeps long before they stain the cabinet.

What replacement involves

If the cabinet floor is swollen, it usually has to be cut out and replaced. Drywall behind it may need drying or removal. After the area is dry, the cabinet, trim and paint can be rebuilt. A scoped plan lists each step.

Sachse notes: plumbing, slabs and water quality

Slab foundations are common in North Texas, and basements are uncommon in Texas according to KUT. In a slab home, the cabinets and closets on the ground floor sit above plumbing in the walls and the slab, so supply and drain leaks usually show up in these spots first.

The City of Sachse maintains water mains and service lines up to your meter. Everything after the meter, including the line under your sink, is the homeowner's to repair. For public water questions, Public Works can be reached at 972-495-7600.

Bringing it together

The repair order matters: stop the source, dry the area, remove what cannot be dried, clean, and only then rebuild. Our water damage repair service covers the rebuild of cabinets, drywall and trim once everything is dry.
