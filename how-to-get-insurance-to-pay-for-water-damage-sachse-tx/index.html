How to Get Insurance to Pay for Water Damage in Sachse, TX

By the Sachse Water Damage Pros teamPublished 2026-10-26Updated 2026-10-26

No guide can promise a claim will be paid, but how you document and report a loss shapes how smoothly it goes. These steps follow Texas Department of Insurance guidance.

Getting insurance to pay for water damage in Sachse starts before you call anyone: stop the water, protect the home and take photos. Then report the loss quickly, keep what was damaged and let the adjuster look before you rebuild. Insurers decide coverage based on your policy and the cause of the loss, so describe what happened plainly and keep every record.

Start with the cause of loss

Coverage depends on why the water arrived. Texas homeowner policies commonly treat a sudden break such as a burst supply line or a failed washer hose differently from a slow leak that went unnoticed. Rising water from outside is another category again and usually needs a separate flood policy.

Before you call, write down what you saw and when. A short timeline helps: when you noticed the damage, what you did to stop it and who you contacted. Avoid guessing about the cause. If you do not know, say so.

Protect the property and keep proof

  • Stop the source. Close the main valve and call a plumber for the repair.
  • Photograph every room. Take wide shots, close shots and the source.
  • Keep damaged items. Hold them for the adjuster until told you may discard them.
  • Dry the structure. Mitigation is a reasonable step to prevent more damage.
  • Save receipts. Emergency services, plumbers and temporary repairs belong in the claim.

Insurers may deny a claim if you do not protect your property, according to the Texas Department of Insurance. That makes drying and tarping part of the process, not an extra.

What documentation helps most

Insurers want to see what was damaged, when and how. Photos with dates, a list of damaged contents and moisture readings from the dry-out all help. A written scope of work from the crew shows what was wet, what was removed and what drying equipment ran.

We provide photos, readings and a written scope that you can send to your insurer. The insurer decides coverage, and we do not promise approval or negotiate the claim for you. If a company says payment is certain, treat that as a warning.

Timelines to watch

Texas law sets deadlines for insurers. Your company has 15 business days to acknowledge a claim and then 15 business days after getting the information it needs to accept or reject it, with extensions in some cases. The deadlines for you are in your policy.

Hidden water damage usually has to be reported within days after you first see it. For policies that pay in two stages, TDI says repairs must be finished within 365 days of the date of loss for the second payment. Mark those dates on a calendar when you file.

Common stumbling blocks

Late reporting, missing photos, discarded items and early rebuilds are the usual problems. Another is describing the cause with a guess. If you do not know why a pipe failed, say so and let the plumber and the adjuster work it out.

Keep communication in writing when you can, and note the date of each call. If a decision confuses you, ask your insurer to explain it in writing.

If you disagree with a decision

Start by asking the insurer to explain the decision and the policy section it relies on. The Texas Department of Insurance publishes consumer guidance and accepts complaints, and its site explains the process. We cannot negotiate a claim for you.
